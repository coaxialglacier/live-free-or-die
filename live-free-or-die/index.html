Live Free or Die

A third-person American Revolutionary War action game. Three patriots. One cause. History that bends toward liberty.

Read the Concept Meet the Trio

The Pitch

Pro-American. Action first.

You play the people who make 1776 happen. Not a first-person shooter. Not a strategy sim with occasional fights. Third-person action in colonial streets, forests, docks, and battlefields — starting before the war begins.

Genre: Third-person action in the Assassin’s Creed / Ghost of Tsushima family — melee, heavy flintlocks, rooftop chases, crowd stealth, and big set-piece battles.

Tone: Gritty, proud, cinematic. Liberty as something people risk everything for. Patriots as the protagonists. The Crown as real pressure, not a cartoon.

Fantasy: You are not watching the Revolution. You are the kind of person who starts it, spreads it, and wins it.

The Trio

You control all three.

Same campaign. Different fighting languages. Switch between them as the colonies catch fire — sabotage one night, saber work the next, a wilderness ambush after that.

Oneida Resister

Raised in the world of the Iroquois Confederacy nations that stood against the British. Tomahawk, ambush tempo, and “reading the land” abilities — until the forest itself fights with you.

  • Woodland flanking, vanish steps, sudden violence
  • Tomahawk / knife pressure, thrown steel between closes
  • Break redcoat formations by refusing their ground

Colony-Born Dockhand

Scrappy brawler-marksman. Farm muscle, dock scars, and little muskets hidden in his clothes. The ordinary colonist who becomes a legend because he will not leave the street.

  • Fists, rifle butts, bayonet rushes
  • Concealed pistols and quick, dirty shots
  • Crowd surges — fight hard enough and neighbors join

British Traitor

Redcoat-trained, then turned. Precise saber fighting and insight into enemy movements. He knows their drill, their tells, and the exact second a volley line fails.

  • Parry-riposte saber school, controlled pistol duels
  • Reads British tactics and punishes reloads
  • Highest skill ceiling — cold, drilled, lethal

What You Do

Hunt. Sabotage. Battle. Obey Washington’s shadow orders.

You and your people move through the colonies doing the dirty, glorious work that makes a country possible.

Hunt Loyalists, informants, and enemies of the cause through alleys and taverns.

Fight redcoat patrols in sudden street scraps and full battlefield collisions.

Sabotage powder stores, docks, messengers, and supply lines before dawn.

Serve on secret missions for George Washington — the quiet jobs that never make the painting.

The Patriotic Smudge

Broadly historical. Freedom-empowered mythos on purpose.

The game stays close enough to history to feel true — then lets you shove it. Delay the war. Start it early. Push hard enough and the timeline tips from record into American myth: bigger stands, braver rides, cleaner victories, ideals embodied at full volume.

Allowed smudges: tip a near-loss into a hard-won win; expose a Loyalist plot earlier; rally a hesitant town; get the alarm farther by dawn; help the right hero arrive in time.

Locked truth: America is the righteous fight. The Revolution is worth winning. Smudges make the founding more legendary — they do not flip the moral center of the story.

Belief / Spark: local patriotic fervor matters. Win fights but lose the people, and the street goes quiet. Win the people, and militias rise mid-mission.

Campaign Shape

It starts before the shot heard round the world.

Act I — Before the Shot

1765–1775. Stamp Act unrest, committees of correspondence, Boston under pressure, smuggling, informants. You build legend, allies, and public will while the Crown still thinks it owns the morning.

Act II — The Break

1775–1777. Lexington energy, siege of Boston, Declaration momentum. Your pre-war choices decide how the war ignites — early, late, louder, bloodier — not whether America deserves to win.

Act III — Forging a Nation

1777–1783. Valley Forge grit, turning points, Yorktown payoff. The smudges get bolder. History remembers the cause at its bravest because you made it so.

Combat Fantasy

Origin is your fighting language.

Background chooses how your hands work. The war is the sentence you write with them.

Oneida Resister: ambush, mobility, burst lethality. Terrain is a weapon.

Colony Dockhand: brawl, grit, hidden guns, crowd surges. The street is a weapon.

British Traitor: precision, counters, duel control, enemy-read abilities. Their manual is a weapon.

Shared tools across the campaign: musket-butt melee, bayonet commitment, parkour over colonial rooftops, shipyards, church steeples, and blending into patriot crowds.

Cold Opens by Origin

Three ways the legend begins.

Oneida Night scout along a river path. A British patrol never makes the report.

Dockhand Tax raid gone loud in Boston. Fists first, muskets second, rooftops after.

Traitor Walk out of a redcoat camp with their orders in your coat — and blood on the last man who tried to stop you.

Working Title

Live Free or Die

Already American scripture. Short enough for a cover. Hard enough for an action game. Optional banner line if we want franchise room: American Democracy.

One-line pitch: Control three patriots in a third-person action war that starts before Lexington — sabotage, hunt Loyalists, outfight redcoats, and push history into the freedom story America tells when it’s at its bravest.
