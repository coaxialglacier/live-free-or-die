# Live Free or Die — Hideout, Underground Network & Raid Loop

Working design for the Robin Hood–adjacent fantasy: hideout workshop, colonial seditious network, raid-to-story progression, and the night the British find the base.

Related: [`../Live-Free-Or-Die-Concept.md`](../Live-Free-Or-Die-Concept.md) · [`story-opening.md`](story-opening.md) · [`plot.md`](plot.md)

> **Feel, not clone.** Same outlaw-band pressure as a Robin Hood strategy adventure. Not the same camera, map mode, or recruiting taverns.

---

## The fantasy in one line

Raid the Crown for the tools you lack, bring them home to people who make them real, use them to force the next chapter of the Revolution… until the Crown follows the smell of your workshop, and you burn your own secrets while fighting in the doorway.

---

## What “Robin Hood feel” means here

Not arrows and green tights. The feel is:

- A small, named band against a system that claims legitimacy
- Winning by humiliating power in public, not only by body count
- Forest, back roads, and night as home ground; town squares as contested ground
- Theft as moral labor: powder, maps, names, and crates leave Crown hands and feed the cause
- Ordinary people as the scoreboard. If they hide you, feed you, or join mid-fight, you are winning
- Legend growing faster than territory. One clean raid makes the next town braver
- Found family of specialists who argue about methods and stay for the same enemy

Mission compass: **Did we make the Crown look stealable, and the people look braver?**

If a mission is only “kill the red square on the map,” it has left this feel.

### Where the game outgrows Robin Hood

Robin Hood often stays perpetual underdog: steal forever, never become the state. This story ends at Yorktown.

Mark the turn on purpose:

| Phase | Band fantasy |
|-------|----------------|
| Act I | Thieves of powder and names |
| Act II | Irregulars who tip battles |
| Act III | The myth catches up; Washington’s quiet jobs become the war’s spine |

Robin Hood feeling early. Founding myth late. Same emotional DNA, different destination.

**Kalha stays his own lane.** Oneida kinship, council, and sovereignty are not “Merry Men with tomahawks.” Colonial underground flavor belongs to Fitch’s world and the cell. Kalha’s plot keeps its own moral engine.

## The hideout is a co-star

Sherwood’s trick is not the trees. It is coming home with loot and watching ordinary people turn stolen Crown goods into tomorrow’s fight.

### What the base is

An **illegal workshop**, not a cozy camp.

Examples by era of the campaign:

- Mill cellar
- River warehouse
- Farm loft network
- Later a colder mobile camp after the first base falls

### Supplies are trophies with jobs

Powder, lead, cloth, nails, ink, horseshoes, British maps, confiscated muskets. Steal it, then watch helpers turn it into cartridges, false papers, shoes, broadsides, repaired arms.

### Villager helpers are Spark made visible

Not quest givers on stools. They are the reason Fitch’s street-rally fantasy feels earned:

- Blacksmith melting British fittings
- Widow sewing cartridge boxes
- Boy running messages
- Printer turning a raid into a ballad

Helpers unlock crafts. They can die or flee if local Belief collapses.

### Emotional loop

1. Raid / smuggle / liberate
2. Dump goods in the hideout
3. See faces light up; hear hammers start
4. Next mission opens with better kit and more hands

Coming home is the loadout altar: suit up at a table, rack, or cellar wall while somebody comments on what you stole. Then the next story letter arrives.

## The trio inside a larger seditious organization

The trio are not the whole Revolution. They are the **sharp edge of a wider conspiracy** that spans colonies by whisper and wagon.

### Working shape

- Local cells: Boston, Concord/Lexington belt, New York docks, Philadelphia printers, frontier paths into Oneida country
- Trio = field operators. Trusted for dirty jobs. Not high enough to know every name
- Fitch already fits: Committee of Safety, arms runner, organizer. He thinks he knows the network until the hanging proves he cannot protect all of it

### Texture

- Codenames, dead drops, tavern passwords, wagon marks
- Rival temperaments inside the cause: printers who want pamphlets, captains who want powder, ministers who want restraint
- Early on they are criminals with a creed; later some cells want Washington’s blessing
- The British defector’s value skyrockets: he reads which “patrols” are hunting the organization

The player should feel: *we are three*, and also *if we fail, a dozen quiet rooms go dark*.

### Multi-colony presence without grand strategy

No continent management spreadsheet. The network talks in letters and shortages:

- Boston needs a ledger
- Philadelphia lost a press
- A southern cell is dry on powder
- You send surplus or go do one violent errand that keeps that cell alive

## Early hanging (not the trio)

Hang a non-trio member early. Someone warm, useful, steadier than the boys. Someone who made the hideout feel like it had a future.

### Candidate roles

| Role | Why it hurts |
|------|----------------|
| Cell quartermaster | Weighed powder; knew every helper by name. Base feels empty after. |
| Courier Fitch almost saved | Flip “the hanging that never happens”: save one, arrive too late for another. |
| Printer-organizer | Public death; broadsides under the scaffold; Belief splits toward silence or rage. |

Working placeholder name from design talk: **Micah** (quartermaster flavor). Lock later.

### Timing

After Lexington chaos, before the trio fully forms. Fitch alone or newly paired with the defector. The organization feels large. One rope makes it mortal.

### What the hanging must do

- Prove the Crown can reach into your world
- Put a name on the informant hunt
- Turn the hideout from clever clubhouse into sacred ground you failed to protect
- Give Spark a bitter version: rage recruits as well as hope
- Make Fitch dangerous. Certainty tips toward brutality. The player sees the cost.

### Preferred staging

Forced witness over clean rescue. Fitch in the crowd, armed, outnumbered. Draw and the cell burns. Hold still and watch. Either way the hideout changes when you come home.

Sample beat:

> They steal a wagon of powder. Helpers cheer. That night British riders take Micah from a safe house because somebody sold a name. Dawn: marketplace scaffold. Fitch in the crowd. Later revenge and informant purges feel earned.

## Campaign loop: raid → base → story (not open world)

**Hub + contracts**, not sandbox.

```
Story mission (pushes plot)
    ↓ reveals target / shortage / threat
Raid mission (steals the missing piece)
    ↓
Home base (craft, equip, helpers react)
    ↓
Next story mission (expects that gear / prep)
```

- **Story missions** = the opera; they push plot and relationships
- **Raids** = short, greedy jobs; optional-feeling in fiction, mandatory under pressure
- **Base** = the breath between them

No wandering a continent for side content. The organization points at a small slate of raids tied to the next story beat. You pick, commit, return, equip, launch.

### Readiness (pressure currency)

Next story mission states needs in plain fiction, not RPG stat blocks:

> “They’ll search every wagon. Without forged dock papers, we go in shooting.”

Raid slate offers 2–3 ways to solve readiness, for example:

- Steal the customs stamp
- Rob a courier for blank passes
- Hit a storehouse for cloth and dye; helpers forge overnight

Pick one path. Return. Equip. Story mission.

## Gear as permission, not cosmetics

Upgrades are only fun when you need them to progress. New kit is a **key**, not +4% damage forever.

| Stolen / crafted piece | What it unlocks |
|------------------------|-----------------|
| Concealed pocket pistols | Start a street mission already inside a Loyalist house |
| Better cartridge quality | Longer sustained fire before militia break |
| Forged passes / redcoat kit | Enter a fort without the loud approach |
| Quieter trail kit / tomahawk balance | Woods ambush routes that do not exist otherwise |
| Stronger powder | Sabotage that actually drops a bridge or magazine |

Rough rule: about **1 in 3** story beats hard-gates on prep. Failure paths can still exist (thin help, partial escape). The player should feel under-equipped, raid, then feel the door open.

Fear → raid → answer:

- Volley lines → better powder / buckshot loads
- Checkpoints → stolen identity
- Woods escorts → quieter arms and land-reading advantage
- Losing another Micah → watchmen and early warning (which makes the later base assault more tragic when it still comes)

## When the British find the base

Mid-game set piece. Earned, not random.

### How they find it (pick one or braid)

- The hanging’s informant trail was not fully burned
- A helper was followed
- A raid went too loud; a survivor described the mill wheel
- The defector’s old regiment triangulates missing reports

### Mission fantasy

1. **Alarm** while mid-prep or returning from a small job
2. **Split objectives:** fight the breach *and* destroy intel
3. **Ugly choices:** save a helper / burn the ledger / hold the powder room / get the wounded out
4. **Collapse:** sanctuary dies in fire and shouting
5. **Aftermath:** mobile camp, paranoia, fewer crafts, harder raids, hotter Spark in towns that heard

Destroying intel should hurt. Names lost. Routes lost. A sister cell goes dark because you burned the only copy to keep it from Gage. Victory is survival with ashes in your mouth.

### Why it refreshes the loop

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| Warm workshop | Colder, meaner mobile camp |
| Clever underground | Hunted underground |
| Upgraded the war | Crown upgraded the hunt |

The organization grows up the night the cellar falls.

## How the pieces lock

```
Hideout cell (helpers + stolen workshop)
    ↑ supplies / recruits
Field jobs (Fitch → + defector → + Kalha)
    ↑ orders / intelligence
Colonial seditious network (other cells, names, rival agendas)
    ↓ pressure
Crown informants + local “Sheriffs”
    ↓
Early hanging of a non-trio member
    ↓
Network goes paranoid; hideout hardens; trio’s bond becomes necessary
    ↓
(mid-game) British find the base
    ↓
Burn intel, fight the doorway, rebuild colder
```

### Local “Sheriff” faces

Gage is too high as the daily villain. Give regions a face for the system: magistrate, customs officer, Loyalist merchant, the lieutenant who almost hanged the courier. Humiliating that face unlocks Spark in that county.

### Ballad logic

After missions, the world retells them. A printer exaggerates. A tavern song lies in your favor. Belief rises because the story traveled. Fits “history bends toward liberty” without needing a strategy map.

---

## Systems touched

- **Belief / Spark:** Helpers, crowds, post-hanging rage, post-raid ballads
- **Alliance / debt:** Still separate for Oneida / Kalha’s people; do not flatten into cell recruitment
- **Loot as theft:** Upgrades come from Crown raids and base craft, not a shop
- **Loadout as trophy:** Guns and kit grow as a museum of British failures
- **Readiness gates:** Story missions expect prep from raids
- **Network paranoia:** After hanging and base fall, fewer names on the wall; some crossed out in red
