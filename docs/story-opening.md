# Live Free or Die — Story Opening

Lexington-first opening. The trio is not together yet. Working draft.

## The game begins at Lexington

The trio does not exist yet.

The player opens as one man: a patriot who has already spent years preparing for the moment everyone else hoped would never come.

His working name is **Elias Fitch**.

Fitch is more than a dockhand or ordinary militiaman. He is a **Committee of Safety operative, arms runner, and local organizer**. His legitimate trade gives him access to wagons, storehouses, stables, river landings, and men who ask few questions. Beneath that respectable life, he moves powder between towns, carries correspondence, identifies Loyalist informants, and helps militia companies arm themselves.

He has probably worked docks and warehouses. That explains his build and fighting style, but it does not define him. His real occupation is building the Revolution before anybody is willing to call it one.

Fitch knows which tavern cellar holds powder. He knows which farmer will hide a fugitive and which magistrate reports to the Crown. When riders bring warning that British regulars are moving toward Concord, Fitch understands immediately what the march means.

The Crown has come to disarm them.

---

## Opening mission: Lexington

The game opens before dawn on April 19, 1775.

Fitch rides into Lexington with a warning, or arrives after moving weapons out of another town. Bells are sounding. Men are leaving houses half-dressed. Some are ready to fight; others still believe the British will posture and continue down the road.

Fitch helps form the militia on the Green. The opening gives the player small actions before combat:

- Wake a militia captain.
- Break open a hidden arms chest.
- Convince frightened neighbors to stand.
- Move women and children away from the road.
- Decide whether to hide valuable powder or distribute it immediately.

Those choices affect how many armed men remain nearby once the regulars appear.

Then the British column comes out of the morning fog.

Fitch recognizes men in that column. He has traded with some of them. He may have drunk beside one of them in Boston. That familiarity makes the confrontation worse. These are soldiers of a government many colonists still consider their own.

An officer orders the militia to disperse.

Nobody knows who fires first.

The opening volley is confused and vicious. Men drop before the player fully understands that combat has begun. Lexington should feel like the world crossing a line accidentally and discovering there is no road back.

Fitch survives the Green, then joins the fighting along the road toward Boston. This is where his combat fantasy emerges:

- He rallies militia who would otherwise scatter.
- He uses stone walls, houses, barns, and fields.
- He fights with a musket, a concealed pistol, and whatever his hands can reach.
- His aggression creates momentum. When he holds his ground, ordinary people begin firing beside him.

By sunset, Fitch is covered in smoke and blood. He began the day as a criminal in the eyes of the Crown. He ends it as an enemy combatant.

That is the birth of the player’s Revolution.

## Fitch after Lexington

Fitch does not immediately receive a mission from Washington. There is barely an army yet.

He spends the next few story beats inside the chaos created by Lexington.

### The names in the coat

Fitch searches a dead British officer and finds a list of suspected Patriot organizers. His own name is on it. So are the names of printers, farmers, riders, and families who helped move powder.

Somebody inside the colonial network has been informing the British.

Fitch’s first post-Lexington mission is to reach those people before Loyalists or British agents do. The player races through towns where news of Lexington is arriving ahead of him. Some inhabitants grab weapons. Others shut their doors.

### The hanging that never happens

A Patriot courier is captured by Loyalists who still expect British authority to return. Fitch attacks the makeshift jail before the prisoner can be transferred.

This shows a dangerous side of him. Fitch believes completely in the cause, but his certainty can become brutality. The player can inspire frightened townspeople or terrify them into obedience. Both may work immediately. Only one builds lasting support.

### The red officer

Evidence points toward a British lieutenant who somehow knew where several militia caches were hidden. Fitch assumes this officer ran the informant network.

He begins hunting him.

The player sees the lieutenant at a distance during one mission. He appears to be directing a search of a Patriot safe house. Fitch cannot reach him before the regulars withdraw. Later, a survivor claims that the same lieutenant quietly left a door unguarded, allowing several Patriots to escape.

Fitch dismisses it as confusion.

The player should also believe this man is an enemy. When Fitch finally corners him, the instinct to kill him should feel justified.

## Meeting the British defector

Several missions after Lexington, Fitch follows the lieutenant to an abandoned mill or riverside storehouse.

Rain outside. Water hammering against the wheel. No army nearby.

Fitch enters expecting a secret Loyalist meeting. Instead, he finds two British soldiers dead and the lieutenant trying to burn a packet of military orders. His red coat is open. His sword is bloody. One of the dead men was his sergeant.

Fitch attacks before asking questions.

The encounter begins as a short duel. The lieutenant is a much better trained swordsman, but he is wounded and refuses several chances to kill Fitch. Fitch eventually knocks the sword away and puts a pistol under his jaw.

> **Fitch:** “You had a fine morning at Lexington. Let me return it.”
>
> **Lieutenant:** “If I wanted your militia dead, they would have been standing where my orders said they were.”
>
> **Fitch:** “They were.”
>
> **Lieutenant:** “No. I changed the map.”

The lieutenant tells him that the British knew about more weapons than they found. For months he had altered reports, delayed searches, and misdirected patrols. Lexington forced his choice. After the shooting, his commanding officer ordered arrests and reprisals against civilians. The lieutenant decided he would no longer disguise obedience as honor.

The dead sergeant discovered him stealing the orders.

Fitch does not believe him until the lieutenant provides proof: the original intelligence report and the false copy he submitted. The false map led British troops toward an empty storehouse. The real map would have exposed one of Fitch’s largest powder caches.

Fitch still wants to shoot him.

The defector says something like:

> “You may kill me. You will feel righteous for an hour. Tomorrow, General Gage will move three companies through a road only I know they intend to use.”

Now the player makes the first major relationship choice:

- Lower the pistol and hear him out.
- Strike him, bind him, and force him to walk ahead.
- Demand that he prove his defection immediately.
- Try to execute him anyway, with outside circumstances interrupting the shot.

The story preserves the defector either way, but the choice determines the foundation of their relationship.

Fitch does not welcome him into the cause. He gives him a temporary stay of execution.

> **Fitch:** “You walk ahead of me.”
>
> **Lieutenant:** “Because you don’t trust me?”
>
> **Fitch:** “Because I want a clean shot.”

## The uneasy pair

Their first mission together is the defector’s test.

He knows that British agents are carrying the names of Patriot organizers to Boston. Fitch knows the local roads and safe houses. Neither can stop the couriers alone.

This mission teaches character switching:

- Fitch rallies local fighters and turns disorder into force.
- The defector predicts patrol behavior and exploits British drill.
- Fitch knows the people.
- The defector knows the machine coming to crush them.

Their personalities should clash constantly.

Fitch believes liberty is a moral fact. The defector believes victory requires discipline, intelligence, and an honest reading of power. Fitch sees caution as cowardice. The defector sees Fitch’s fury as something the British can manipulate.

Their friendship begins when each man witnesses the other refuse an easy betrayal.

Perhaps Fitch has a chance to hand the lieutenant over to enraged militia and refuses because he gave his word. Later, the defector can escape during combat but returns to save Fitch. Neither discusses it afterward.

## Bringing Kalha into the story

Kalha enters after the pair has survived enough together to become functional but not comfortable.

The orders they intercept reveal that British agents are moving through Oneida country. Their purpose may be to secure scouts, map routes, pressure leaders, or inflame existing conflicts so the Crown can present itself as the only stabilizing power.

Fitch wants the intelligence stopped because it threatens the Patriot network. The defector understands that British attention to the frontier means a larger military plan is forming.

Kalha attacks the same operation for his own reasons.

The first meeting should involve confusion. Fitch sees an Oneida warrior striking the British party and assumes he is an unexpected ally. The defector warns that they have no idea who Kalha is fighting for. Kalha has no reason to trust either of them: one is a loud colonial revolutionary, and the other is still wearing pieces of a British uniform.

They cooperate because British reinforcements arrive.

Afterward, Kalha makes the difference clear:

> “I did not come to help your rebellion. I came because those men carried the names of my people.”

Fitch respects that answer. The defector understands its political meaning.

Kalha joins them provisionally, just as the defector did. The trio forms through overlapping interests before it becomes brotherhood.

## Early story spine

```
Lexington
  → Fitch becomes a wanted revolutionary
  → rescue Patriot names from British/Loyalist agents
  → hunt the redcoat officer behind the intelligence
  → discover that the officer is defecting
  → uneasy Fitch/defector missions
  → intercepted British operation leads into Oneida country
  → meet Kalha during a three-sided fight
  → trio forms provisionally
  → Kalha receives the Catawba call
  → Fitch and the defector choose to follow him
  → Catawba battle
  → Oneida welcome and local alliance
```

### Relationship origins

- Fitch and the defector begin with a pistol under the defector’s jaw.
- Kalha and the others begin with separate motives in the same battle.
- The Catawba mission is where the new trio first chooses one member’s people over their own immediate objectives.

That choice turns three useful allies into “the boys.”
