# Live Free or Die — Enemy Cast (Spit Names)

Working bestiary for Crown and Loyalist opposition. Halo grammar (each type changes your next three seconds). Names you can snarl — period slang, kit metonyms, real corps — not org-chart wallpaper.

Related: [`../Live-Free-Or-Die-Concept.md`](../Live-Free-Or-Die-Concept.md) · [`combat.md`](combat.md) · [`combat-pressure.md`](combat-pressure.md)

---

## Laws

**Every enemy type must change what the player does with their next three seconds.**

If swapping the model wouldn’t change your plan, it isn’t a type yet.

**If a dockhand could snarl the name after a tavern fight, keep it.**  
If it sounds like a spreadsheet row (“elite officer unit”), kill it.

**Difficulty is composition, not sponge HP.** Meaner mixes. Less forgiveness. Lobsterbacks never become tanks.

**Ranks and biomes still matter.** A town problem and a forest problem are different animals even at similar heat. See [`combat-pressure.md`](combat-pressure.md).

---

## The spit-name bar

**Lobsterbacks** set the standard: red coat, seafood insult, period mouth, perfect.

Hessians, Jägers, Queen’s Rangers, and Mohawk/Seneca war parties already pass muster as proper nouns. They need entrance music, not cute renames.

## Core loop cast

### Lobsterbacks

Patriot mouth-name for ordinary redcoats. The chorus in scarlet.

Soft alone. Holy when dressed. Your basic King’s meat — until a better bastard starts conducting them. Break their count, steal a loaded Bess, make the lobster boil into panic.

If we need a Halo grunt, this is him — named by the people who hated him.

### Bloodybacks

Same army, uglier soul. Period twin-insult: the lash, the scars, the men flogged stiff enough to hold a line when nice boys won’t.

These are the keepers behind the Lobsterbacks (file-closer energy without the clipboard name). Kill a Bloodyback and the chorus forgets the song. Leave him up and your smoke only buys a second verse.

Traitor should spot Bloodybacks the way a butcher spots the joint.

### Light Bobs

Real slang for light infantry. Fast, rude, angle-thieves.

They take doorways, garden walls, orchard lips. They punish peeking and feed Settle Meter without charging stupid. Smoke them, flank them, do not play whack-a-mole with their shoulder forever.

### Bearskins

Grenadier silhouette as the name. Tall caps. Bayonet religion.

They walk through the white cloud you paid for with thunder. When someone yells “Bearskins!” you stop admiring your shot and start finding steel.

### Gorgets

Officers named after the throat-bit of brass that says *I own this street.*

Pistols. Hangers. Orders that invite worse things — flanks, runners, Grasshoppers. Some are duelists. Some are cowards with good lungs. Cut the Gorget before he finishes calling for the iron.

### Sheepskins

Drummers. The skin on the drum.

Tiny bodies, huge authority. While a Sheepskin lives, Lobsterbacks can be restarted like a machine. Silence him and the street loses its metronome. Halo energy: this small thing is ruining my life.

## Place and punishment spice

### Grasshoppers

Period nickname for light battalion guns — the little jump-and-spit artillery that can appear where a siege monster can’t.

Camping in the wrong town doesn’t always mean fortress cannon. Sometimes it means a Grasshopper kissing your stone wall into gravel. Cute name. Mean toy.

### Matrosses

Real word: artillery privates, the powder-black hands of the Ordnance.

If they finish their work, cover ends. If you butcher them mid-setup, you stole a thunder god from the Crown. Highest-value breach targets when the train is coming.

### Jollies

British nickname for Marines. Harbor teeth.

Rope, tar, pistols in a narrow hold. When a dock raid goes loud too long, the Jollies are who come laughing up the gangway — historically honest flood for waterfronts, not an inland regiment jogging out of nowhere.

### Gallopers

Road knives on horse. Farm lanes, messengers, the backside of your clever wall.

One Galloper escaping is tomorrow’s garrison. One at your spine is why sitting still on a stone wall feels stupid. Lonely-road Settle Meter without fake infantry printers.

### Cowboys

De Lancey’s Neutral Ground wolves. Westchester cattle-and-night energy. “Outlaw of the Bronx” shadow.

If you turtle, they don’t bring a cannon. They take the living that feeds your hideout. Parish lawlessness Lobsterbacks don’t have. Fitch should want them dead in a particular way.

### Addressers

Loyalists who signed addresses of loyalty — parish men with ink on their hands and your friends on a list.

Soft clothes. Hard evenings. The civil-war whisper class. Fight too static in town and Addressers are why helpers vanish two streets over.

### Catchpoles

Old word for the arrest-men. Provost energy with a spit-name.

Irons. Carts. The scaffold’s errand boys. When Catchpoles enter the scrap, the mission becomes *who leaves free.* Ties straight into hanging and hideout wounds.

### The Silk

The Colours. One word.

Men die stupid around it. Steal it and towns catch fire in the heart. Leave it coming and Lobsterbacks stay mythical too long. Not every fight — special encounter ingredient.

### Forlorn hope

Already a poem. Period term for the first through the breach.

A shape of attack more than a standing class: doorway stormers, mill-gate smashers, the ugly tip of a bayonet hour. When the Crown sends a forlorn hope, the doorway becomes a mouth.

## Named curses (campaign spice)

Proper nouns players learn to dread. Entrance music, not renames.

| Name | Mood |
|------|------|
| **Queen’s Rangers** | Simcoe’s green coats. Smart knives. Not parade fools. |
| **Butler’s Rangers** | Niagara frontier dread. Pocketing, burning what you left behind. Wyoming/Cherry Valley weather in their shadow. |
| **Johnson’s Greens** | King’s Royal Yorkers. Mohawk Valley exile grudge with addresses. |
| **Tarleton’s Legion** | Green jackets, horse and foot, rumor of no quarter. Southern terror mood. |
| **Skinner’s Greens** | New Jersey Volunteers marauder taste. Local maps, civil hatred. |
| **Hessians** | Rented German weight. Different songs, different bayonet manners. |
| **Jägers** | Hunter-riflemen. Patience with a German edge. They take your tree and charge rent. |
| **Fraser’s** | Highlander storm as a proper growl. Pipes as tell. |
| **The Guards** | Brigade of Guards — London pride, expensive competence. Rare good silver. |
| **Ferguson’s Rifles** | Rare breechloading “what is that gun?” problem. Trophy if taken. |
| **Emmerick’s Chasseurs** | Mongrel specialist corps around New York lines. |
| **Mohawk / Seneca war parties** | Peer forest nations in the Longhouse fracture. Contest Kalha’s woods. Never fodder. Never cosplay. |

## How a street should talk

> “Bloodyback’s down — Lobsterbacks are meat.”  
> “Light Bobs on the bakery cut.”  
> “Bearskins in my smoke.”  
> “Gorget’s calling a Grasshopper.”  
> “Quiet the Sheepskin.”  
> “Catchpoles on the cart — move.”  
> “Green coats — Queen’s Rangers. Don’t sit.”

If players talk like that, the cast is alive.

- **Traitor:** Gorgets and Bloodybacks are his language; he hears the script and tears pages out
- **Kalha:** Jägers, Butler’s, Mohawk/Seneca contests; pocket them before they pocket you

## Silhouettes and audio IDs

Must read at a glance through fog and smoke:

- Lobsterback: standard line shape
- Bloodyback: broader furniture, louder body language behind the file
- Light Bob: door-peek posture, lighter kit
- Bearskin: tall cap, bayonet already committed, forward lean
- Gorget: cleaner lines, pistol, saber, space around him
- Sheepskin: small, drum, dangerous to ignore
- Matrosses / Grasshopper: crew cluster + carriage oddness
- Jollies: sea-soldier kit, harbor rooms
- Cowboys / Addressers: civilian-military hybrid, not parade
- Named greens / Hessians / Highlanders: distinct coat and audio world

If smoke erases identity, add call-outs, whistles, German drill, pipes, officer bark.

## Success test

Mute the UI. Drop the player into a yard.

They should say, without prompts:

> “Bloodyback first, smoke the Light Bobs, steal from the Lobsterbacks, don’t let the Gorget finish calling the Grasshopper.”

If they only say “shoot the red ones,” the cast isn’t done.
