## Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy

Six Nations (the Longhouse):

| Nation | Role in Revolution (general) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Mohawk | Mostly British |
| Oneida | Mostly Patriot |
| Onondaga | Often tried neutrality; many British-leaning |
| Cayuga | Mostly British |
| Seneca | Mostly British |
| Tuscarora | Mostly with Oneida / Patriot |

The Great Peace bound the Six Nations to each other. Outside that circle, the Confederacy was an expansionist military power for generations: mourning wars, captive-taking, tribute, and conquest.

### External warfare (context for Oneida ruthlessness)

In the Beaver Wars and after, Haudenosaunee campaigns smashed or scattered peoples including Wendat (Huron), Erie, Neutral, Petun, Susquehannock, and Mahican; pressed Algonquin nations; pushed into the Ohio country (Illinois, Miami, Shawnee); and held Delaware (Lenape) under the Covenant Chain as tributaries.

Into the 1700s, a long feud with the **Catawba** (Carolinas) meant raids and counter-raids over great distance — war parties, running battles, assaults on towns — not only woodland ambush.

## Oneida Nation

- Homeland: upper Mohawk Valley / central New York (People of the Standing Stone — Onʌyoteˀa·ká).
- Clans include Wolf, Bear, Turtle (among others).
- Important towns in the Revolutionary era: **Kanonwalohale**, **Oriska**, and other castles.

### Why many Oneida leaned Patriot

- Trust and contact through missionary **Samuel Kirkland** and patriot neighbors.
- Anger over British land pressure, including the **1768 Treaty of Fort Stanwix**, which pushed a boundary deep into Oneida land.
- Practical judgment that settlers would remain neighbors either way — and that the Crown used Native allies hard, then poorly repaid them.
- Still: early policy was **neutrality**, not instant alliance.

### Named historical figures (reference, not playable cast)

Use as texture and council NPCs; do not overwrite real biographies with the player character.

- **Han Yerry / Hanyery (Tewahangarahken)** — “He Who Takes Up the Snow Shoe”; Wolf Clan war leader; Oriskany; later captain’s commission.
- **Tyonajanegen** — “Two Kettles Together”; fought at Oriskany, loaded for Hanyery when wounded.
- **Good Peter (Agwirondongwas / Lakwilotákwas)** — speaker, warrior-chief tradition.
- **Shenandoah (Oskanondonha)** — major Oneida leader.
- **Polly Cooper** — went with Oneida to Valley Forge; taught corn preparation; oral tradition of the shawl.
- **Ojistalak (“Grasshopper”)** — Turtle Clan sachem / Odatseghte tradition.

## Oneida–Catawba conflict

Use for the early alliance mission (see plot doc).

- Long-running northern Iroquois vs Catawba hostility through the 18th century.
- Typical forms: long-distance war parties, “cutting-off” raids, **running battles**, hits on aggregated / palisaded towns.
- Example texture: mid-1700s accounts of Catawba pursuing northern raiders and fighting multi-day running fights; Catawba reputation as hard fighters among Carolina and Virginia observers (including Washington’s world).

Game use: a **daylight meeting fight** or **palisade assault**, not another ambush (ambush is reserved for the later British crate mission).

## Deliberations: British, Patriot, or neither

This is the historic backbone of the post-battle council sequence.

### Timeline

| When | What |
|------|------|
| **June 1775** | Oneida council at **Kanonwalohale**. Neutrality declaration. |
| **June 19, 1775** | Message to Connecticut (via Kirkland): will not join King or Colonies; quarrel of “two brothers.” |
| **1776** | Six Nations Grand Council declares / tries neutrality; unity frays. |
| **May 1776** | Many other Iroquois lean or pledge British; Oneida resist that pressure (“dread the consequences”). |
| **Summer 1776** | Oneida, Tuscarora, and others form a defensive understanding to stand with Americans if the rest join the King. |
| **Jan 1777** | Council fire at Onondaga covered; each nation chooses its own path. |
| **Aug 6, 1777** | **Battle of Oriskany** — Oneida fight with Patriot militia against British, Loyalists, Mohawk, Seneca; civil war inside the Longhouse goes loud. |
| **1777–78** | Oneida scouts/warriors with Gates; contingent to Valley Forge; later Barren Hill under Lafayette. |

### Neutrality declaration (core lines to echo in dialogue)

From the Oneida statement (Kirkland transcription), June 1775:

> We cannot intermeddle in this dispute between two brothers… We are unwilling to join either side… Should the great King of England apply to us for our aid, we shall deny him. If the Colonies apply, we will refuse… We are for peace.

Signed / spoken for by sachems, warriors, and **female governesses** (clan mothers). Represent the whole nation in the scene.

### Arguments that belong in the council

1. **Neutrality** — unnatural English civil war; stay out; keep Six Nations from being pulled apart.
2. **Crown** — Covenant Chain, trade, British power that beat France; fear of rebel weakness.
3. **Colonies** — Kirkland/neighbor trust; Stanwix land grievance against British dealing; future lived among settlers.
4. **Longhouse cost** — choosing means fighting Mohawk/Seneca kin.
5. **Survival** — whoever wins will remember who stood where.

### Internal Longhouse war (later texture)

Oriskany and after: Mohawk (often under **Joseph Brant / Thayendanegea**) and Seneca vs Oneida/Tuscarora. Village burnings both ways (e.g. Oriska hit after Oriskany; Oneida retaliation on Mohawk towns). Do not flatten this into “Iroquois vs outsiders” only — by 1777 the wound is also inside the Confederacy.
